How it works

Experience someone else’s experience by physically going where it happened.

The magic is simple: the message is not fully accessible until the user reaches the location. That turns passive content into real-world discovery.

1

Create a memory

A person, museum, or business writes, records, or publishes a story tied to a real location.

2

Tag the place

The creator pins it to precise GPS coordinates so the message belongs to that place forever.

3

Visit in real time

Users walk there, stand in that space, and unlock the post once they are physically present.

4

See the hidden layer

They discover a memorial, a memory, a museum reconstruction, or a recommendation that changes how they see the place.

FAQ

Questions partners and users will ask first.

This section helps you handle early objections during museum outreach and live demos.

Why does the content unlock only on-site?

Because that is what makes the experience feel real. EchoEarth turns a place into part of the message. The person must physically arrive to fully experience what was left there.

Why would museums care?

It expands their reach far beyond their walls, creates new educational experiences, and allows them to teach history exactly where it happened.

Can multiple layers exist in one place?

Yes. That is the core concept: one location can hold public memories, legacy messages, museum exhibits, and commercial recommendations at the same time.

How does the platform make money without ads?

Through museum licensing, cultural passes, institutional partnerships, educational experiences, and optional premium preservation features.
