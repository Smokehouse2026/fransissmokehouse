Where It Started

Highway 61
Runs Through Everything

The Francis Smokehouse sits on US Highway 61 — the old Blues Highway that carries history through the heart of Louisiana. When the doors opened in 2015, the mission was simple: cook honest food the way it was meant to be cooked. No shortcuts. Real wood. Real smoke. Real patience.

What started as a passion for Louisiana pit BBQ grew into something the community claimed as their own. A place regulars return to every week, and visitors drive across the state to find.

The Method

Low & Slow
Is the Only Way

Every rack of ribs, every brisket, every pulled pork shoulder starts the same way — seasoned, loaded into the pit, and left alone. Real smoke takes time. You cannot rush hardwood.

The result is a smoke ring that goes deep. Bark that holds. Meat that pulls cleanly. Beyond the BBQ, the kitchen extends into the Louisiana Cajun tradition — fresh boudin, crawfish pies, and egg rolls that bring the bayou straight to the table.

The Francis Smokehouse fires up the pit for the first time on Highway 61 in St. Francisville. Word travels fast down the road.

Locals start returning weekly. The corn pudding becomes legendary. The menu grows to include po-boys, Cajun specialties, and rotating chef specials.
